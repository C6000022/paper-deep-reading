# Generic Critical-Reading Note Structure

Use this generic structure unless the local private profile provides a more specific version. Unverified fields must be marked as unspecified rather than completed from general knowledge.

```markdown
# Deep reading: <paper title>

## 1. Bibliographic information

## 2. Research background

## 3. Study subjects, materials, data, and conditions

## 4. Research methods

## 5. Principal results

## 6. Mechanism or interpretation

## 7. Key conclusions

## 8. Novelty

## 9. Limitations and potential problems

## 10. Relationship to the user's research

## 11. Final assessment
```

## Required analysis

### 1. Bibliographic information

Record the title, authors, journal or venue, publication year, persistent identifier when present, study object, and central research question.

### 2. Research background

Identify the problem, limitations of prior work, and the stated hypothesis or objective. Distinguish the authors' explicit claims from the analyst's synthesis.

### 3. Study subjects, materials, data, and conditions

Record the study system, inputs, composition or dataset, groups and controls, key variables, preparation or sampling, relevant dimensions, environmental conditions, time points, and exposure conditions. Use tables for concrete data when useful.

### 4. Research methods

For every reported method, state its purpose, timing, essential parameters, measured outputs, and how the authors use the result. Do not claim that an unreported method was used.

### 5. Principal results

Organize results in a causal or evidentiary sequence appropriate to the field. For every principal result, provide the trend, representative value when reliably available, comparison between groups, PDF page and figure or table reference, and the authors' explanation. Avoid unquantified descriptions when the paper provides quantitative evidence.

### 6. Mechanism or interpretation

Separate direct observations, author interpretations, and analyst inferences. Identify direct support, speculation, plausible alternatives, and gaps in the evidence chain. Do not convert correlation into causation.

### 7. Key conclusions

Extract five to ten important conclusions when the evidence supports that number. Include the conditions and scope of each conclusion and avoid generalizing beyond the study.

### 8. Novelty

Assess the paper's claimed contributions in the study system, method, environment, or conceptual understanding against its evidence. Do not repeat promotional claims from the abstract without evaluation. Distinguish a new observation from a demonstrated mechanism. Unless an independent literature comparison has been explicitly requested and performed, state that field-wide novelty and priority remain unverified; absence of prior work from the supplied paper is not proof that none exists.

### 9. Limitations and potential problems

Assess controls, replication, uncertainty analysis, confounding, representativeness, support for mechanism claims, extrapolation, missing tests, and omitted factors. Separate limitations admitted by the authors from those identified by the analyst.

### 10. Relationship to the user's research

Use only the research-context sources configured in the local profile. Identify conclusions that may be cited, methods that may be borrowed, parameters that may be referenced, content that cannot be transferred directly, and open questions worth studying. If context is inaccessible or insufficient, say so.

### 11. Final assessment

Rate the paper's research value as high, medium, or low with reasons; identify the most important figures, tables, or sections; and state the three points worth remembering.

## Evidence rules

- Cite important evidence using the physical PDF page number, counting the first file page as page 1. Do not use printed article pagination.
- Include a section, figure, or table identifier when available.
- Do not fabricate missing information or units.
- Show calculations and label derived values.
- Identify the exact location of locally unreadable content.
- Keep quotations short and separate from explanation.
- Distinguish paper facts, author interpretations, and analyst inferences.
